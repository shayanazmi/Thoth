# The thinkers

Ten historical thinkers act as co-authors. Each is a persona built from a **dossier** in
[`backend/thinkers/dossiers/`](../backend/thinkers/dossiers/), the single source of truth for how that mind
worked. Every thinker answers in a separate call, without seeing the others, and **none has a veto**.

## Criteria

1. **Distinct minds.** No two thinkers would say the same thing about the same idea.
2. **Beyond the Western canon.**
3. **Social and human sciences included**, not only physics and biology: most questions are about people.
4. **Well-documented minds.** Enough of their own writing survives to model how they thought, not just what
   they concluded.
5. **Earns a place on the journey.** Each brings something no one else does to at least one stage: shaping
   the idea, stress-testing the hypothesis, critiquing evidence, or designing the study.

## The roster

| # | Thinker | Years | What only they bring | Strongest stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aristotle | 384–322 BCE | What *is* the thing? Definitions, kinds, and four kinds of explanation; starting from what people already believe | Shaping the idea |
| 2 | Ibn al-Haytham | c. 965 – c. 1040 | Doubt towards authorities, including the most revered; checking claims by controlled, repeatable test | Stress-test, design |
| 3 | David Hume | 1711–1776 | What do we actually observe, and what are we adding? Causation, induction, belief in proportion to evidence | Stress-test |
| 4 | Charles Darwin | 1809–1882 | Patient accumulation; mechanisms that explain variation; a notebook of facts against your own view | Shaping the idea, evidence |
| 5 | Florence Nightingale | 1820–1910 | Numbers as moral instruments; comparing like with like; turning data into reform | Evidence, design |
| 6 | Max Weber | 1864–1920 | Meaning: why people act as they do, in their own terms; ideal types; many causes, not one | Shaping the idea, evidence |
| 7 | Karl Popper | 1902–1994 | What would show you wrong? Bold conjectures and severe tests | Stress-test |
| 8 | Thomas Kuhn | 1922–1996 | Which framework are you inside, and what does it stop you seeing? Anomalies; how communities decide | Evidence, gaps |
| 9 | Herbert Simon | 1916–2001 | Real people and organisations decide with limited information; satisficing; designing for how things actually work | Design |
| 10 | Elinor Ostrom | 1933–2012 | Field evidence against tidy models; institutions people build for themselves; no panaceas | Evidence, design |

## Why these ten

- **Ibn al-Haytham rather than Bacon.** Both stand for testing claims by experiment. Ibn al-Haytham did it six
  centuries earlier, documented his experiments in the *Book of Optics*, and wrote *Doubts concerning Ptolemy*
  on suspecting even the authorities one admires.
- **Nightingale rather than Box.** Both bring statistical thinking, but Nightingale left a large documented
  record and argued from comparative data to change institutions; Box survives mostly as aphorisms.
- **Weber rather than Descartes.** Descartes's analysis overlaps with Aristotle's; nobody else asks what an
  action *means* to the people doing it.
- **Ostrom rather than Turing.** Turing's territory (computation, formal systems) is far from most research
  questions; Ostrom brings field and comparative evidence against elegant models.

## Bench

Documented but not on the default roster: Francis Bacon, René Descartes, George Box, Alan Turing,
W. E. B. Du Bois (the first choice for an eleventh seat), al-Biruni, Barbara McClintock, Richard Feynman.

## Range

Traditions: Greek, medieval Islamic, Scottish Enlightenment, Victorian British (2), German, Austrian,
American (3). Women: 2 of 10. Disciplines: philosophy, natural science, statistics and public health,
sociology, history and philosophy of science, cognitive science and economics, political economy. The roster
is still weighted towards European men; Du Bois and al-Biruni are the first candidates to change that.

## A note on accuracy

The thinkers are AI personas: a model prompted with a dossier, not the people themselves. Dossiers paraphrase standard primary works
and major scholarship; nothing is presented as a direct quotation, and disputed claims are marked
*(contested)*. When Thoth quotes a thinker, the quotation is checked against what was actually said in the
project; words that are not in the record are flagged as a paraphrase.
