# Historical Basis: René Descartes & Foundational Decomposition

## 1. Primary Sources
* **Descartes, René (1637)**: *Discours de la méthode pour bien conduire sa raison, et chercher la vérité dans les sciences* (*Discourse on Method*).
* **Descartes, René (1641)**: *Meditationes de Prima Philosophia* (*Meditations on First Philosophy*).
* **Descartes, René (1684 posthumous / written c. 1628)**: *Regulae ad directionem ingenii* (*Rules for the Direction of the Mind*).

## 2. Core Methodological Principles
1. **The Four Rules of the Cartesian Method** (*Discourse*, Part II):
   - *Evidence / Doubt*: Accept nothing as true that is not clearly and distinctly presented to the intellect without occasion for doubt.
   - *Division / Decomposition*: Divide each complex difficulty into as many parts as possible and requisite for its adequate solution.
   - *Order / Synthesis*: Conduct thoughts in ordered succession, commencing with objects simplest and easiest to know, ascending step-by-step to knowledge of the composite.
   - *Enumeration*: Make enumerations so complete and reviews so general as to be certain of omitting nothing.
2. **Methodological Skepticism as Stress-Testing Tool**: Systematically suspending inherited, unverified, or conventional premises to isolate indubitable foundations.
3. **Dependency Cascades**: Recognizing that a deductive chain is only as robust as its weakest foundational link; if an early assumption is invalidated, all downstream propositions collapse.
