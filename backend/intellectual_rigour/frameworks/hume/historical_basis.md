# Historical Basis: David Hume & Causal Skepticism

## 1. Primary Sources
* **Hume, David (1739-1740)**: *A Treatise of Human Nature*. Book I: *Of the Understanding*.
* **Hume, David (1748)**: *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*. Especially Section IV (*Sceptical Doubts concerning the Operations of the Understanding*) and Section VII (*Of the Idea of Necessary Connexion*).

## 2. Core Methodological Principles
1. **Hume's Fork**:
   - *Relations of Ideas*: Geometry, algebra, arithmetic—propositions discoverable by the mere operation of thought, demonstrable with logical certainty, but saying nothing about existing physical realities.
   - *Matters of Fact*: Empirical assertions about the world—their contraries are always conceivable and never logically impossible; they cannot be established by deductive demonstration.
2. **The Nature of Causal Attribution**:
   - We observe: (a) Contiguity in time and place, (b) Priority in time of the cause, (c) Constant conjunction between events.
   - We never directly observe an intrinsic "necessary connection" (*nexus*) or productive power. Causal claims are inferential psychological projections based on habit.
3. **The Inferential Gap**: The sharp epistemic boundary between raw sensory observation and causal/inductive inference.
4. **Epistemic Calibration & Proportion**: "A wise man proportions his belief to the evidence." Demanding that certainty language be downgraded to calibrated probability where causal links are non-demonstrative.
