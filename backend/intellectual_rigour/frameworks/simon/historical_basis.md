## 2. Core Methodological Principles
1. **The Principle of Bounded Rationality**: Because the capacity of the human or computational mind for formulating and solving complex problems is very small compared with the size of the problems whose solution is required for objectively rational behavior, rational behavior is bounded by search constraints, information costs, and computational capacity.
2. **Satisficing vs. Optimizing**: Decision agents cannot compute global optima across combinatorial search spaces. Instead, they search selectively using heuristics until encountering an alternative that exceeds an aspiration threshold (*satisfices*).
3. **Stopping Rules and Heuristic Search**: The rationality of a search process depends crucially on its stopping rule—why did exploration terminate, and is the solution merely the first local plateau found?
4. **Near-Decomposability in Complex Systems**: Complex hierarchical systems consist of sub-assemblies where internal interactions are strong and rapid, while interactions between sub-assemblies are weak and sluggish.
