# Historical Basis: Francis Bacon & Eliminative Induction

## 1. Primary Sources
* **Bacon, Francis (1620)**: *Novum Organum Scientiarum* (*The New Organon*). Detailed methodology for inductive science replacing Aristotelian syllogism.
* **Bacon, Francis (1605)**: *The Advancement of Learning*. Systematic classification of sciences and identification of cognitive impediments.

## 2. Core Methodological Principles
1. **The Three Tables of Comparative Discovery**:
   - *Table of Presence*: Cases where the investigated nature is present.
   - *Table of Absence in Proximity*: Cases most similar to presence cases where the phenomenon is unexpectedly absent.
   - *Table of Degrees/Comparison*: Cases where the magnitude of the phenomenon varies with candidate factors.
2. **Induction by Elimination (*Inductio per Rejectionem et Exclusionem*)**: Affirmation can only occur after all competing explanatory factors have been systematically excluded through negative instances.
3. **Crucial Instances (*Experimentum Crucis*)**: Decisive observations or experiments designed specifically where two competing theories make incompatible predictions.
4. **Doctrine of the Four Idols (*Idola Mentis*)**:
   - *Idols of the Tribe*: Inherent cognitive biases of human nature (pattern-seeking in randomness, confirmation bias).
   - *Idols of the Cave*: Individual predispositions, specialty dogmas, and narrow research habits.
   - *Idols of the Marketplace*: Vagueness, jargon inflation, and words that refer to nonexistent entities.
   - *Idols of the Theatre*: Uncritical dogmatic acceptance of reigning philosophical or methodological systems.
